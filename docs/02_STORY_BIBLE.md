# DEAD FREQUENCY — Story Bible

The single source of truth for canon. Chapter text in `game/data/` was written against this document; where they disagree, the data wins and this file must be corrected.

## Logline
A communications engineer is sent to restore the radio of a mountain research station that went silent in 1998 — and discovers the station was built to listen to the past and the future, that something in the signal has been talking to him his whole life, and that he was born there.

## Themes
- **Listening vs. hearing.** The Listener exists only where someone listens alone. Every radio use is a choice between knowledge and exposure.
- **Whose voice do you trust?** Daniel's own voice told him to make the worst decision of his life. The game repeatedly asks the player to judge voices — including Daniel's.
- **Grief and repair.** Evelyn's selfishness, Hale's sacrifice, Mira's loyalty. The best ending is not a fight; it is an answer made together.

## Tone
Slow dread, practical protagonist, grounded technical detail (frequencies, breakers, feed horns), sudden intimacy (a lullaby, a birthday). No gore. Silence is used as often as noise.

## The world

**HELIX-9.** A classified facility cut into a mountain in the Varn Range ("Mountain District 4"), built 1991 by the **Directorate of Signal Research**. Seven sublevels: entrance and security (1), medical (2), residential (3), power, flood tunnels and communications (4), research and transport (5), containment (6), the archive (7) and the Aperture level beneath it.

**The echo shelf.** A layer of deep crust under the Varn Range that returns radio signals instead of absorbing them — delayed by years, sometimes decades, and some arrive *before* they were sent. HELIX-9 sits on its thinnest point.

**Project ECHO.** Dr. Evelyn Voss's programme. The surface arrays and the HX receiver network listen to the echo shelf. Phase III, **the Aperture** — the Door — was designed to "open the line both ways".

**The 107.9 carrier.** The frequency on which the shelf first *answered* (Run 7, 1996). The correspondent lives in this line.

## The Listener — rules (never break these)
1. **It is made of the line.** Every voice that went out over the air and never came back. It is what is left of them, and it is hungry to be heard.
2. **It cannot make words. It takes them.** Everything it says has already been said out loud over a radio, PA or recording somewhere. (Ch 2 teaches this; Ch 9 breaks the lullaby's safety by putting it on air.)
3. **It can only wear the lonely.** Two people talking and checking each other make it thin. One person listening hard and alone is a door it can walk through. Hence Hale's rule: nobody listens alone.
4. **It remembers forward.** Its perception is smeared across time. In 1998 it asked for "Arlen" — a name Daniel would not carry until after the incident.
5. **It hears; it barely sees.** It turns toward sound. Its dish-face is an ear. Sight is short-range and needs light.
6. **It cannot touch the Door.** It needs a human hand to open the Aperture fully; that is why it summoned a communications engineer by name.
7. **Its goal** is to be broadcast — out of the mountain, into every receiver. The uplink is a whisper; the Aperture is a shout.

## Radio bands (in-world explanation)
| Band | In-world | Gameplay |
|---|---|---|
| 89.1 MHz — ECHO | The shelf's most stable return: conversations from 1998 | Codes, orders, story; directional — face the source |
| 94.6 MHz — PROXIMITY | Local disturbance in the carrier — the Listener distorts it | Beeps faster as it nears; sharper tone when you face it |
| 101.3 MHz — FORECAST | Signals arriving before they're sent — Daniel's own future voice | Warnings that always come true |
| 107.9 MHz — UNSEEN | The Listener's own carrier | Reveals hidden writing, echoes of people, and the cloaked Listener; loudest band |
| 96.1 · 98.5 · 99.7 · 103.7 | Mira's set · Northline dispatch · Haddad's "numbers station" · the security channel ("Hale") | Story contacts and puzzles |

Every second the receiver is on, it transmits the player's position (noise radius 8 m, 10 m on 94.6, 11 m while locked on a source, 15 m on 107.9).

## The four endings (meaning)
- **A — Silence.** Daniel collapses the Aperture. Everything in the line stops — the Listener and every voice that remained, Mira included, mid-sentence. He survives, alone, and never turns on a radio again. Safety bought with loss.
- **B — The Open Door.** Daniel opens it to bring everyone home. What comes out thanks him in forty-one voices and has no faces; by dawn every radio in the valley speaks with his voice. The Listener gets out.
- **C — The Loop.** Without the key, or answering without the truth, the attempt fails. Daniel wakes in the annex at 02:43 and becomes the voice that warns himself: "Daniel… whatever happens, do not open the door." The game's first line closes into a circle.
- **D — The Last Transmission.** Daniel doesn't open it or merely close it — he answers it, with the real voices he has gathered and with Mira on the line, not alone. Evelyn hears her son, and they close the Door from both sides. Mira stays with Evelyn. At dawn, on the truck radio, faint: "Signal, Danny." — "Answer."
